Autonomous green-powered water cleaning boat

A boat that cleans floating waste from the water on its own, so the Nile and Egypt's waterways stay clean.

Graduation project by a team of seven students

Who we are

We are seven students working on our graduation project. Our team covers planning, research, simulation, electronics and implementation. We share one goal: protecting Egypt's waterways with clean, practical technology.

The problem

Floating waste in canals, rivers and the Nile harms water quality, wildlife and the people who depend on the water.

  • Polluted waterways

    Plastic and other trash gather on the surface and keep spreading downstream.

  • Slow manual cleaning

    Cleaning by hand or with crewed boats takes a lot of time, effort and people.

  • Costly and polluting

    Fuel-powered cleaning vessels are expensive to run and add emissions of their own.

  • Hard to reach

    Some areas are difficult or unsafe for workers to reach regularly.

How HAPI solves it

HAPI is an autonomous boat that collects floating waste from the water surface, using clean energy and low-cost electronics.

  1. It works on its own

    The boat moves and cleans without a crew on board.

  2. Smart control

    An ESP32 microcontroller controls the boat's movement and electronics.

  3. Powered by batteries

    The prototype runs on batteries, with solar power planned as a future upgrade.

  4. Designed and simulated first

    We designed and simulated the circuits in Proteus and planned the build with a block diagram.

Our goal

A cleaner Nile and a brighter future, with waterway cleaning that is cheaper, faster and greener.

  • Reduce floating waste without putting workers at risk.
  • Replace fuel-based cleaning with clean energy.
  • Build a working prototype and grow it with solar power.
  • Work with partners. The Suez Canal Authority has shown interest in the idea.
